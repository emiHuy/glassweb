Tracker categories

Every captured request is checked against the Disconnect.me tracker dataset. Matches are grouped into these categories.

Advertising
Domains that display or enable ads and marketing offers, capable of tracking personal information and exposing users to risk even without direct interaction.
Analytics
Trackers that collect user information to build behavioral profiles based on online activity, which can be connected to real names or unique identifiers.
Anti-fraud
Tools whose explicit purpose is to prevent or detect fraud, provided they do not use third-party context data (like IP addresses and user identifiers) for any purpose outside of safety operations.
Consent Managers
Services whose explicit and primary purpose is to manage consent preferences, restricted from utilizing third-party context data to enable tracking.
Content / CDN
Infrastructure serving fonts, scripts, or media. Usually not tracking-focused, but still a third party.
Cryptomining
Domains that cause the user's browser to mine cryptocurrencies without explicit user opt-in.
Email
Trackers commonly embedded in emails or webmail interfaces to monitor engagement.
Email (Aggressive)
Email tracker domains serving images or links that, when blocked, may alter expected functionality or break webmail/email rendering.
Fingerprinting (General)
Trackers that use browser or device features and properties in unintended ways to identify and track a particular user or device without using cookies.
Fingerprinting (Invasive)
Trackers that exploit APIs to extract information about a user's computing environment when those APIs were not designed to expose such data.
Social
Trackers used by social networking services to monitor web browsing activities across third-party websites and apps, even when you aren't logged in or visiting the social network itself.
Unclassified
Not found in the tracker dataset. This means unrecognized, not confirmed safe.
